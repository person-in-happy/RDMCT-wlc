\title{Two-stage MILP scheduling and structure-aware learning-enhanced
branch-and-cut for dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber cluster tools}

\author{Anonymous author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber cluster tools coordinate product
wafers and reusable vacuum-zone dummy wafers across rotating chambers, load
locks, and shared robots. We formulate a finite-horizon two-stage MILP that
jointly determines $4\times1$/$2\times2$ recipe grouping, chamber and slot
assignment, transfer, cleaning, and dummy-wafer allocation. Stage one
minimizes makespan; stage two retains its makespan and discrete structure
while refining timing. RDMCT-HBS accelerates branch-and-cut using a
structure-preserving binary-skeleton
(SPBS) warm start, a 23-dimensional variable-role representation,
A3C-inspired parallel policy-gradient training, bounded beam decoding, and
role-submodular completion. Four verification cases reach zero gap and pass
independent checks. In a 2700-run nominal matrix, RDMCT-HBS attains the lowest
mean primal--dual integral
($1.6586\times10^4$) and solve time (218.32~s) among seven methods, reducing
mean PDI by 1.41\% relative to adapted HEM and 1.97\% relative to SCIP. In a
960-run experiment, SPBS raises incumbent availability from
51.67\% to 100\% and reduces mean PDI by 26.19\%; both effects remain
significant after Holm adjustment. A 1500-run controlled study shows that
the full two-stage profile reduces event-reconstructed mean route wait by
51.91\%, maximum route wait by 68.89\%, cadence CV by 39.70\%, and
cadence-deviation sum by 94.05\% relative to disabling refinement; all four
comparisons remain significant after Holm adjustment. Results establish
advantages of the learning-enhanced search and two-stage scheduling.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item A two-stage MILP represents dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber operations.
\item Conditional refinement preserves makespan and reshapes schedule timing.
\item SPBS cuts mean PDI by 26.19\% and raises incumbent availability to 100\%.
\item Role features and structure-conditioned beam decoding extend adapted HEM.
\item RDMCT-HBS records the lowest observed mean PDI and time in 2700 nominal runs.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Semiconductor manufacturers face high product variety, stringent process
requirements, and strong pressure to obtain more capacity from installed
equipment. Because capacity expansion is capital intensive, better operational
use of existing tools remains an economically relevant alternative to adding
hardware \citep{chien2021drlhga,hong2026ddpgct}. Cluster tools are central to
this objective because they integrate process modules, load locks, and robotic
handlers within a tightly coupled environment. Their productivity is governed
not only by nominal process durations but also by the coordination of material
release, chamber occupancy, transport, cleaning, and temporal restrictions.

This study considers a dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber cluster tool
deployed in a plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) process
scenario. Product wafers arrive from load ports, whereas reusable vacuum-zone
dummy wafers are supplied from a dedicated vacuum-zone dummy-wafer storage
area. The two flows meet in shared rotating chambers and must be coordinated
through atmospheric and vacuum robots and two load-lock paths. Mixed
$4\times1$ and $2\times2$ recipes further couple product grouping, chamber and
slot assignment, dummy-wafer availability, cleaning boundaries, and residency
limits. A locally attractive transfer or processing order may consequently
block several downstream resources. Operational complexity therefore arises
from the propagation of decisions across the complete equipment, not from an
isolated machine sequence.

Analytical and optimization-based cluster-tool studies have made substantial
progress on steady-state cycles, multiple wafer types, chamber cleaning, and
residency-time control
\citep{kim2021integrated,ko2021wafer,qiao2022binary,lee2023cleaningplan,
li2022conditioncleaning,wang2023optimalcyclic,li2025cyclicmultitype,
yang2025miptwowafers}. These models clarify important local mechanisms, but
their system assumptions do not jointly represent the finite-horizon
dual-source flow, mixed rotary recipes, dummy-wafer reuse, and shared transport
architecture considered here. The multi-finger, multi-space model of
\citet{gu2024multifinger}, for example, evaluates predetermined steady-state
robot sequences, whereas the present problem must determine discrete
assignments, resource orders, and continuous event times simultaneously.

Learning-based scheduling provides a complementary research stream.
Deep reinforcement learning has been combined with genetic or adaptive search
for dynamic semiconductor and cluster-tool decisions
\citep{chien2021drlhga,liu2022cnna3c,zhang2024qeda,
kim2025adaptivecluster,kim2025noncyclic}. Particularly relevant is the
simulation-integrated DDPG study of \citet{hong2026ddpgct}, which adjusts
continuous process times in a physical vapor deposition (PVD) cluster tool.
That study demonstrates the value of linking equipment behavior with learned
control, but its decision layer differs from the present one: the PVD policy
regulates process duration under a simulation model, while this paper
constructs a feasible discrete rotating-chamber schedule and retains exact MIP bounds and
proof mechanisms. The two problems are thus complementary rather than
interchangeable.

An explicit mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) formulation is attractive
for this scheduling problem because it exposes the physical assumptions and provides
primal and dual bounds. The same fidelity, however, creates many assignment
and resource-order binaries and weakly coupled timing variables. The resulting
branch-and-cut search can be sensitive to which valid inequalities are
retained at each separation round. Learning can guide this decision without
replacing the solver's feasibility and proof responsibilities
\citep{bengio2021machine,scavuzzo2024mlbb}. HEM formulates cut retention as
hierarchical cardinality and ordered-subset decisions
\citep{wang2023hem,wang2024hemtpami}; nevertheless, its generic features do
not identify how a cut distributes mass across variable types and
objective-active continuous variables, greedy
decoding commits to early choices, and an exact solve may spend substantial
time before finding a competitive incumbent.

Taken together, the literature leaves an unresolved interface between an
operationally faithful model for dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber equipment and a
scalable exact solution mechanism that exploits the model's structure. We
address the modeling and computational gaps jointly: the first part of the
framework describes the equipment through a two-stage MILP, and the second
uses variable-type/objective-role information together with feasible schedule
structure to enhance HEM within SCIP. The resulting logic is
equipment physics $\rightarrow$ exact scheduling model $\rightarrow$ structural
search bottleneck $\rightarrow$ learning-enhanced branch-and-cut.

The contributions are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A finite-horizon two-stage MILP is developed for dual-source
  mixed-flow rotating-chamber cluster tools. It jointly represents product and dummy-wafer flows,
  mixed $4\times1$/$2\times2$ recipes, rotary chamber events, ATR/VTR
  transfers, load-lock states, cleaning epochs, residency limits, and
  vacuum-zone dummy-wafer storage and reuse. Stage one minimizes makespan; stage two performs
  makespan-preserving timing refinement conditional on the retained discrete
  structure.
  \item A structure-aware enhanced HEM method is developed for this MILP.
  A structure-preserving binary-skeleton (SPBS) warm start supplies an early
  feasible incumbent; a reconstructed 23-dimensional representation exposes
  discrete and continuous variable roles; and A3C-inspired parallel policy
  training, bounded beam decoding, and role-submodular completion coordinate
  cut cardinality, membership, and order.
  \item A paired computational study provides complementary evidence for the
  model, the completed algorithmic components, and manufacturing difficulty.
  Small exact instances assess model validity;
  the complete nominal matrix compares seven solver configurations across
  independent solver and training seeds; controlled stability and factorial
  studies isolate the two-stage mechanism and manufacturing difficulty.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of the paper reviews the two research streams and positions the
present study, formulates the rotating-chamber scheduling model, develops the
structure-aware solution method, reports the computational study, and
concludes with operational implications and research limitations.

\section{Literature review and research positioning}
\label{sec:literature}

\subsection{Cluster-tool scheduling under mixed flows and auxiliary constraints}

Cluster-tool scheduling has evolved from steady-state robot cycles toward
models that account for product heterogeneity and auxiliary equipment
constraints. Integrated patterns and parallel-chamber workload balance have
been studied for dual-armed tools and multiple wafer types
\citep{kim2021integrated,ko2021wafer,zhu2022twotypes}. More recent work
coordinates multi-type concurrent processing or formulates two-wafer-type
single-arm scheduling as a MIP
\citep{li2025cyclicmultitype,yang2025miptwowafers}. These studies establish
that release order, chamber allocation, robot tasks, and residency restrictions
cannot be optimized independently when several product types share a tool.

Cleaning and other temporal restrictions add another coupling layer.
Binary formulations and analytical methods have been developed for
residency-constrained tools with cleaning
\citep{qiao2022binary,li2022conditioncleaning,lee2023cleaningplan}.
Purge, post-processing, and processing-time variation also change feasibility
and cycle behavior
\citep{yuan2023purge,zhu2023postprocess,pan2024cycletime}. At the equipment
level, processing-module configuration and robot waiting must be coordinated
\citep{wang2023optimalcyclic}; multi-space modules introduce additional
rotation and loading interactions \citep{zou2024twospace,gu2024multifinger}.
The dominant focus nevertheless remains a single material source, a cyclic
regime, or a predetermined robot pattern. These assumptions are restrictive
for the finite-batch rotating-chamber system considered here, where product wafers and
reusable vacuum-zone dummy wafers follow different routes before meeting in shared rotary modules.

\subsection{Learning-based semiconductor and cluster-tool scheduling}

Learning-based manufacturing scheduling increasingly combines a fast policy
with problem-specific search. At the fab or production-system level,
\citet{chien2021drlhga} integrate deep reinforcement learning with a hybrid
genetic algorithm, while \citet{zhang2024qeda} use Q-learning to guide an
estimation-of-distribution search. CNN--A3C has been used for semiconductor
scheduling under uncertain processing times and urgent orders
\citep{liu2022cnna3c}; feedback and actor--critic control have likewise been
combined to exploit production-system knowledge
\citep{li2022hybridfeedback}. Problem-specific encodings and greedy search
remain relevant because learned policies alone do not guarantee effective
local improvement \citep{hu2023crowsearch}.

At the cluster-tool level, DRL with adaptive search has been used for
concurrent release and robot-task decisions
\citep{kim2025adaptivecluster}, and a related study addresses noncyclic
scheduling directly \citep{kim2025noncyclic}. \citet{hong2026ddpgct} combine
discrete-event simulation and DDPG to control continuous processing times in
a PVD tool. The latter is the closest CIE study in equipment-level motivation,
but it addresses a different control lever. Its policy changes process
duration in a simulated PVD environment; the present policy allocates valid
cuts while an exact solver determines a discrete rotating-chamber schedule. This
distinction motivates learning inside, rather than in place of, mathematical
optimization.

\subsection{Learning-enhanced exact optimization and research gap}

MILP makes route, capacity, cleaning, and residency assumptions inspectable,
although formulation choices can materially alter relaxations and search
\citep{adelgren2023recordkeeping}. Learning-enhanced exact optimization
preserves this modeling role while guiding selected solver decisions
\citep{bengio2021machine,scavuzzo2024mlbb}. Learned variable biases can support
primal search \citep{khalil2022mipgnn}, and graph representations can encode
variable--constraint interactions
\citep{cappart2023gnnco,chen2023milpgnn}. SCIP provides the modular
branch-and-cut interface used in this study
\citep{achterberg2009scip,bestuzheva2023scip}.

Within branch-and-cut, cut selection has been learned through reinforcement
learning, supervised ranking, and look-ahead imitation
\citep{tang2020cut,huang2022cutranking,paulus2022looking}.
Instance-adaptive rules further demonstrate that a fixed scoring vector is not
uniformly effective \citep{turner2023adaptive}. HEM jointly predicts retained
cardinality and an ordered cut subset
\citep{wang2023hem,wang2024hemtpami}, while beam search provides a general
mechanism for retaining alternative partial sequences
\citep{choo2022beam}. These methods establish the algorithmic ingredients,
but they do not connect an application-feasible incumbent and
variable-type and objective roles to hierarchical cut decoding.

\section{Dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber system and two-stage MILP formulation}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Equipment architecture and assumptions}

The investigated equipment is a finite-batch rotating-chamber cluster tool with separate
atmospheric and vacuum domains. Product wafers follow the route
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{LP}&\rightarrow\mathrm{ATR}\rightarrow\mathrm{AL}
 \rightarrow\mathrm{ATR}\rightarrow\mathrm{LL_U}\rightarrow\mathrm{VTR}\\
 &\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}\rightarrow\mathrm{VTR}\rightarrow\mathrm{LL_L}
 \rightarrow\mathrm{ATR}\rightarrow\mathrm{LP}.
\end{aligned}
\]
where LP denotes a load port, ATR and VTR are the atmospheric and vacuum
transfer robots, AL is an aligner, and $\mathrm{LL_U}$ and $\mathrm{LL_L}$ are
the upper and lower load locks. Vacuum-zone dummy wafers travel from the
vacuum-zone dummy-wafer storage area to
the shared process chambers through the VTR. Chambers CH2 and CH3 rotate and
share transfer and loading resources.

Two processing modes are present. A $4\times1$ rotary batch contains two
physical sides; the front side is loaded first and unloaded last, while the
back side is loaded second and unloaded first. A product-deficient tail side
is filled by a pair of dummy wafers. In $2\times2$ mode, product-wafer pairs form an
ordered chain bounded by leading and trailing dummy-wafer pairs, and each product pair
participates in two adjacent exposures. A cleaning boundary splits the chain
at an exposure boundary and converts the chamber to a dummy-wafer-only state.
Each vacuum-zone dummy wafer is reusable only after its individual load-to-unload occupancy
intervals end. Robot moves and incompatible loading operations are
non-overlapping.

Processing and transfer times are deterministic, all wafers are available at
the beginning of the finite horizon, and preemption is not allowed. Each
activated operation is assigned to exactly one compatible chamber or slot
group. The model optimizes assignments, mode choices, cleaning epochs, and
resource orders jointly.

\subsection{Sets, parameters, and variables}

Let $\mathcal W$ be the product wafers and $\mathcal P^4$ and
$\mathcal P^2$ the product-pair sets prescribed for the two recipe families.
Let $\mathcal C=\{\mathrm{CH2},\mathrm{CH3}\}$, $\mathcal B_c$ be candidate
$4\times1$ batches, $\mathcal Q_c$ be $2\times2$ chain positions,
$\mathcal E_c$ be cleaning-separated process epochs, and $\mathcal T_c$ be
the reusable vacuum-zone dummy wafers assigned to chamber $c$. The complete event set is $\mathcal O$,
the physical resources are $\mathcal R$, and $\mathcal A$ contains
technological precedence arcs.

Binary $x^4_{pcbh}$ assigns $p\in\mathcal P^4$ to chamber $c$, batch $b$, and
side $h\in\{F,B\}$; $x^2_{pcq}$ assigns $p\in\mathcal P^2$ to chain position
$q$. Variables $u^4_{cb}$ and $u^2_{cq}$ activate batches and chain
positions, $a_{vce}$ assigns process cycle $v$ to epoch $e$, and
$g_{j\tau}$ assigns dummy-wafer operation $j$ to physical dummy wafer $\tau$. Binary $y^r_{oo'}$ orders
two actions on shared resource $r$. Every action $o$ has start $s_o$,
completion $e_o=s_o+d_o$, and $\cmax$ is the latest product return to an LP.

\subsection{Stage-one makespan model}

Every prescribed product pair is assigned once:
\begin{align}
\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_c}
\sum_{h\in\{F,B\}}x^4_{pcbh}&=1
&&p\in\mathcal P^4, \label{eq:assignment-four}\\
\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_c}x^2_{pcq}&=1
&&p\in\mathcal P^2. \label{eq:assignment-two}
\end{align}
With filler indicator $f_{cbh}$, active $4\times1$ sides and contiguous
batch/chain positions satisfy
\begin{align}
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P^4}x^4_{pcbh}+f_{cbh}&=u^4_{cb}, \label{eq:fill}\\
u^4_{c,b+1}&\le u^4_{cb},&
u^2_{c,q+1}&\le u^2_{cq}. \label{eq:contiguity}
\end{align}
Additional tail equalities permit $f_{cbh}=1$ only on the last active batch.

For every action, $e_o=s_o+d_o$. Product and dummy-wafer routes impose
\begin{equation}
s_{o'}\ge e_o+\delta_{oo'},\qquad(o,o')\in\mathcal A,
\label{eq:precedence}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{oo'}$ includes rotation, pressure transition, or mandatory
empty travel. Two actions sharing unit-capacity resource $r$ obey
\begin{align}
s_{o'}&\ge e_o+\rho^r_{oo'}-M(1-y^r_{oo'}),\\
s_o&\ge e_{o'}+\rho^r_{o'o}-My^r_{oo'},
\qquad o,o'\in\mathcal O_r. \label{eq:resource-disjunction}
\end{align}
This form covers ATR, AL, VTR, stateful load-lock slots, and chamber
occupancy. Endpoint-dependent $\rho^r$ accounts for robot repositioning.

Rotary equalities enforce front load, rotation, back load, immediate process,
back unload, rotation, and front unload for each $4\times1$ batch. In
$2\times2$, adjacent exposures share the intermediate product pair and a
bridge action replaces the outgoing pair by the incoming pair. All active
$4\times1$ batches on a chamber precede its $2\times2$ block.

Let $H$ be the maximum process cycles between cleans and let $z_{ce}$ activate
epoch $e$. Process-cycle assignment and cleaning capacity satisfy
\begin{align}
\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_c}a_{vce}&=u_{vc},\\
\sum_v a_{vce}&\le Hz_{ce}, \label{eq:cleaning-capacity}\\
s^{\mathrm{clean}}_{c,e+1}&\ge E^{\mathrm{last}}_{ce},
\label{eq:cleaning-order}
\end{align}
where $E^{\mathrm{last}}_{ce}$ is a linear completion envelope. Linking
constraints insert a dummy-wafer-only cleaning batch between consecutive epochs and
allow a $2\times2$ chain to split only at an exposure boundary.

Each dummy-wafer operation selects one chamber-bound reusable dummy wafer,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T_c}g_{j\tau}=1,\qquad
j\in\mathcal J_c^{\mathrm{DW}},
\label{eq:dummy-wafer-assignment}
\end{equation}
and occupancy intervals assigned to the same physical dummy wafer $\tau$ are disjoint by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:resource-disjunction}. Product and robot residency limits have
the form
\begin{equation}
0\le s_{\mathrm{out}(w,r)}-e_{\mathrm{in}(w,r)}
\le\overline R_{wr}. \label{eq:residency}
\end{equation}
Finally,
\begin{equation}
\cmax\ge e^{\mathrm{return}}_w\quad(w\in\mathcal W),\qquad
\min_{z\in\mathcal F}\cmax(z),
\label{eq:stage1}
\end{equation}
so the primary objective covers the full route back to the LP. The feasible
set $\mathcal F$ contains the assignment, route, resource, rotation, cleaning,
residency, load-lock-state, and dummy-wafer availability constraints.
\subsection{Makespan-preserving conditional refinement}

Several membership, slot, and alias variables are fixed to zero or one by
mode-induced equalities. Declaring such variables continuous while retaining
their fixing equalities removes redundant integrality declarations without
changing their values or the projection of $\mathcal F$. Genuine assignment,
mode, ordering, and cleaning decisions remain integer.

Let $\widehat z$ be the best stage-one solution,
$\widehat C=\cmax(\widehat z)$, and
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fix}}$ the discrete decisions retained in the second
stage. Let $\varepsilon_C\ge 0$ denote the makespan-preservation tolerance.
The refinement imposes
\begin{equation}
 z_d=\widehat z_d\quad(d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fix}}),\qquad
 \cmax(z)\le\widehat C+\varepsilon_C,
 \label{eq:incumbent-fix}
\end{equation}
while selected double-gripper and continuous timing decisions remain free.
It minimizes
\begin{equation}
 S_{\mathrm{lin}}(z)=
 \alpha_w\sum_{i\in\mathcal W}w_i+
 \alpha_m w^{\max}+
 \alpha_c\sum_{j\in\mathcal C}\delta_j+
 \alpha_e\sum_{k\in\mathcal E}e_k,
 \label{eq:stability}
\end{equation}
where $w_i$ are waiting or idle quantities, $w^{\max}$ is the largest
individual wait, $\delta_j$ are absolute deviations between successive start
cadences, and $e_k$ are soft wait-cap excesses. The weights are fixed across
all compared methods. If stage one proves optimality, Eq.~\eqref{eq:incumbent-fix}
preserves the globally optimal makespan. If stage one terminates with a
feasible incumbent but without a proof, stage two preserves that incumbent
makespan; its stability optimum is conditional on the fixed discrete
subspace.

\subsection{Model scope and induced computational structure}

The formulation differs from sequence-specific steady-state LPs in both
horizon and decision scope. It optimizes a finite batch, treats product wafers
and vacuum-zone dummy wafers as interacting material streams, and determines grouping,
assignment, cleaning, transport order, and event times within one model.
The second stage does not claim a global lexicographic optimum over every
stage-one-optimal discrete schedule: it refines timing conditional on the
discrete structure retained from stage one. This distinction protects the
primary makespan while keeping the refinement computationally interpretable.

Operational fidelity is also the source of computational difficulty.
Assignment, activation, cleaning-epoch, dummy-wafer, and resource-order binaries
interact with several classes of continuous timing variables. Consequently,
two cuts with similar generic efficacy may constrain very different physical
parts of the schedule. The next section uses this variable-type/objective-role
information to design the warm start, cut representation, and decoding
procedure.

\section{Structure-aware enhanced HEM for exact solution}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Integrated solution architecture}

The proposed method targets the two principal symptoms of the formulation:
late incumbent discovery and structurally heterogeneous cut pools. The warm
start addresses the primal trajectory, whereas the enhanced HEM policy
allocates the cut budget using information exposed by the formulation and
solver metadata.
These mechanisms remain subordinate to the exact solver and therefore do not
alter the feasible region.

The solution sequence is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \text{SPBS incumbent}&\rightarrow\text{hierarchical cut policy}
 \rightarrow\text{beam/role completion}\\
 &\rightarrow\text{SCIP branch-and-cut}
 \rightarrow\text{two-stage schedule}.
\end{aligned}
\]
SPBS and learned cut selection target different parts of the primal-dual
trajectory. SPBS seeks an early feasible incumbent, whereas the learned
selector allocates a limited cut budget during separation. In the principal
method comparison every algorithm receives the same precomputed SPBS solution.
End-to-end time includes loading and injecting that solution, all callback
work, and SCIP solving. This common-start design holds the primal-start
mechanism fixed in the nominal comparison so that the compared configurations
differ in cut selection and decoding.

\subsection{Structure-preserving binary-skeleton warm start}

Directly fixing a complete schedule can impose a brittle heuristic path. SPBS
instead fixes a small interpretable binary skeleton in a temporary model and
uses SCIP to complete the remaining timing and resource decisions. For each
binary variable $x_j$, it constructs a one-hot role vector
\[
 z_j\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal H_s|},\qquad
 \mathcal H_s=\{A,U,E,L,R_{\ell},R_h,O\},
\]
where $A$ denotes wafer or batch assignment, $U$ activation, $E$ cleaning or
process epochs, $L$ load-lock slots, $R_{\ell}$ low-risk local orders, $R_h$
high-impact VTR orders, and $O$ residual binaries.

Each role has a confidence and a fixing rule. Assignment, activation,
cleaning-epoch, and slot variables have high confidence because they follow
from the recipe grammar. ATR, aligner, and load-lock orders have intermediate
confidence. VTR order is more influential and is released in the relaxed SPBS
profile. For temporary model $\widetilde M$, a skeleton value $\widehat x_j$
is imposed only when the role/profile rule selects it:
\[
 \ell_j=u_j=\widehat x_j
 \quad\text{if}\quad
 \pi(z_j,\kappa_{h(j)},\text{profile})=\text{fix}.
\]
Continuous event times, waiting variables, and unfixed resource orders remain
free.

The skeleton follows the recipe physics. Complete $4\times1$ rotary batches
are filled first. In $2\times2$ mode, product pairs form contiguous exchange
chains bounded by dummy-wafer events. When a chain reaches a cleaning trigger, the
next admissible event converts the chamber to a dummy-wafer-only state before
cleaning, after which product exchange resumes. A feasible completion is
passed to the original model through SCIP's primal-solution interface. No SPBS
constraint is added to the original solve, so its feasible set and proof
requirements are unchanged.

\subsection{Candidate interface and reconstructed 23D features}

At separation round $t$, SCIP supplies a set $\mathcal C_t$ of valid candidate
inequalities. An efficacy prefilter retains at most 128 candidates and the
selector returns no more than 16. Cut coefficients and right-hand sides are
never modified.

The adapted HEM representation contains 13 generic measurements: objective
parallelism, efficacy, support, integral support, normalized violation, and
four statistics each for cut and objective coefficients. To reconstruct the
interaction between cut geometry and variable-type roles, define
\[
 \mathcal G=\{B,I,C_{\mathrm{obj}},C_{\mathrm{aux}},O\},
\]
for binary, general-integer, objective-active continuous, continuous
auxiliary, and unclassified variables. For candidate row
$\sum_j a_{ij}x_j\le b_i$, its coefficient mass on role $g$ is
\begin{equation}
 p_{ig}=
 \frac{\sum_{j:\,x_j\in g}|a_{ij}|}
      {\sum_j|a_{ij}|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}}.
 \label{eq:role-mass}
\end{equation}
Here $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}>0$ is a numerical stabilizer. Role assignment
uses SCIP variable metadata and is invariant to variable renaming.

The coupling term is zero for a cut supported entirely on one side of the
discrete--continuous split and is largest for balanced support. Dominant mass
and entropy distinguish focused cuts from cuts mixing several roles. Together
with the generic features, the candidate representation has 23 dimensions.

\subsection{A3C-inspired parallel hierarchical policy gradient}

The hierarchical pointer policy \citep{vinyals2015pointer,wang2023hem} first
predicts retained cardinality $k_t$ and then an ordered cut sequence:
\begin{equation}
 \pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)=
 \pi_{\theta_c}^{\mathrm{count}}(k_t\mid s_t)
 \prod_{\ell=1}^{k_t}
 \pi_{\theta_p}^{\mathrm{ptr}}
 (i_\ell\mid s_t,i_{1:\ell-1}).
 \label{eq:hierarchical-policy}
\end{equation}
Both policies use a 128-dimensional hidden representation and one pointer
glimpse. The reward is the negative SCIP-reported primal-dual integral,
\begin{equation}
 R=-\pdi_{\mathrm{SCIP}},
 \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
so larger reward represents a better anytime primal-dual trajectory.

Independent worker processes evaluate complete SCIP trajectories in parallel.
Their policy gradients are aggregated before a centralized update. With
exponential moving-average baseline
\begin{equation}
 b_{q+1}=\beta b_q+(1-\beta)\overline R_q,
 \label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
the estimator is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\nabla_\theta J}=
 \frac{1}{B}\sum_{\ell=1}^{B}
 (R_\ell-b_q)\nabla_\theta
 \log\pi_\theta(a_\ell\mid s_\ell).
 \label{eq:policy-gradient}
\end{equation}
This organization follows the parallel exploration and shared-policy
principle of A3C \citep{mnih2016a3c}. The actual estimator in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:policy-gradient} is specified by the centralized update and EMA
control variate; throughout the paper it is termed A3C-inspired parallel
hierarchical policy gradient.

\subsection{Beam decoding and role-submodular completion}

Training samples ordered subsets stochastically. At evaluation, the adapted
HEM baseline uses greedy pointer decoding, whereas the beam variants retain
the three highest-scoring partial sequences. End-of-sequence handling,
candidate pools, and cut caps are identical across decoding modes. Beam and
callback computation are charged to the end-to-end limit.

The neural order can concentrate its budget on one structural pattern. The
first $\lceil\gamma k_t\rceil$ candidates are therefore retained as anchors
$A_t$, and an expansion pool $R_t$ of at most $\alpha k_t$ candidates is
formed by appending rows with high normalized structural quality. For
$S\subseteq R_t$, define
\begin{align}
F_t(S)={}&\sum_{i\in S}(0.35q_i+0.20r_i)
+0.25\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}d_g
\sqrt{\sum_{i\in S}p_{ig}}\nonumber\\
&+\frac{0.20}{|R_t|}
\sum_{u\in R_t}\max_{i\in S}s_{ui},
\label{eq:submodular}
\end{align}
where $q_i$ is normalized structural quality, $r_i$ the neural-rank prior,
$d_g$ role demand in $R_t$, and $s_{ui}\in[0,1]$ clipped cosine similarity
between generic-role descriptors. The first term preserves quality and neural
order, the concave term gives diminishing returns to repeated role mass, and
the facility-location term rewards representation of the candidate pool.

Starting from $A_t$, the method repeatedly adds the candidate with largest
marginal gain until $k_t$ cuts are selected. For fixed anchors and expansion
pool, the nonnegative modular, concave-over-modular, and facility-location
components are monotone submodular. Cardinality-constrained greedy completion
therefore obtains at least a $(1-1/e)$ fraction of the best residual gain
inside $R_t$ \citep{nemhauser1978analysis}. This bound concerns residual set
completion, not branch-and-cut runtime or final MIP quality. The implementation
uses $\alpha=2$ and $\gamma=0.25$.

\subsection{Solver validity and computational accounting}

\method{} ranks only inequalities already generated and validated by SCIP. It
does not generate constraints, modify coefficients, prune nodes, or declare
infeasibility. Unselected candidates remain available according to the solver
interface. Thus the learned component can change search order and runtime but
not the scheduling feasible region or the validity of an eventual proof.
Feature computation, device transfer, policy inference, beam decoding, role
completion, SPBS loading, and SCIP solution time are included in end-to-end
time. The common SPBS solution is loaded before each nominal solve; its
construction time is outside the nominal online timing.

\subsubsection{Training and computational environment}

Each policy configuration is trained with five independent random seeds for
60 epochs. The lower-level policy uses Adam with learning rate $10^{-4}$,
batch size eight, maximum gradient norm two, and eight training samples per
epoch. The high-level count policy uses learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$. Two
sample processes collect trajectories in parallel. The learning rate is
multiplied by 0.96 every ten epochs. Candidate and selected-cut caps are 128
and 16.

Experiments use SCIP 10.0.1 through PySCIPOpt 6.1.0 and Python 3.12.12.
Policy inference uses PyTorch 2.11.0 with CUDA 13.0 on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX
5060 Ti. The host runs Windows 11 on an Intel Core Ultra 7 265KF with 20
physical cores and 31.7~GB RAM. Nominal, factorial, SPBS, and sensitivity
solves use a 600-s wall-clock limit and a 2048-MB SCIP memory limit. Each
solver invocation is single-threaded.

\subsubsection{Performance measures and statistical analysis}

The primary measure is the SCIP primal-dual integral (PDI); lower values
represent faster joint improvement of primal and dual bounds. Secondary
measures are incumbent availability, final primal bound, final relative gap,
optimal-solution rate, nodes, callback overhead,
stage-specific status, stability measures, and end-to-end time. A time-limited
solve remains informative through its incumbent, bound, gap, and PDI.
For every saved stability-study schedule, physical route waiting is
reconstructed from six nonnegative event gaps per product wafer: before
alignment, before upper-load-lock transfer, after the upper load lock, before
processing, after processing, and after the lower load lock. Their sum and
maximum define route-total and route-maximum wait. Cadence CV and the sum of
absolute consecutive cadence changes are reconstructed from the start times of
active chamber batches within each cleaning epoch. This event-time definition
is identical for all three stability profiles and excludes secondary-objective
slack variables.

Comparisons are matched by manufacturing instance and solver seed and, for
learned methods, training seed. Repeats are first aggregated within a
manufacturing instance so that the manufacturing instance is the statistical
unit. Principal comparisons include paired differences, bootstrap 95\%
confidence intervals, two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests where applicable,
and Holm-adjusted $p$-values. Completed component contrasts report matched
mean differences and Holm-adjusted $p$-values. Incumbent and failure rates are
reported alongside continuous metrics. This instance-centered design treats
heterogeneous MIP cases, rather than repeated solver seeds, as the units of
generalization \citep{gleixner2021miplib}.

\subsection{Operational interpretation and limitations}

The proposed framework separates three responsibilities. Manufacturing
engineers specify verifiable route, chamber, cleaning, and dummy-wafer constraints;
SCIP generates valid inequalities and retains responsibility for mathematical
proof; and the learned component allocates a limited cut budget. This
separation is valuable when schedule feasibility cannot be delegated to a
black-box predictor. The two-stage model also separates throughput-oriented
makespan from operational smoothness: the second stage is designed to reduce
avoidable waiting and cadence variation without sacrificing the retained
stage-one makespan.

In the complete nominal matrix, \method{} leads all seven configurations in
mean PDI and solve time under the evaluated protocol. Its nominal
instance-level intervals include zero, indicating that the gain varies across
instances. Independently, the SPBS experiment establishes large,
multiplicity-adjusted improvements in incumbent availability and PDI, while
the controlled two-stage study provides multiplicity-adjusted evidence for
the scheduling mechanism.
Wall-clock comparisons are hardware-dependent, and feature extraction, SPBS
loading, and beam decoding remain in the common online time budget. The role
representation depends on the variable
taxonomy exposed by SCIP, and the $(1-1/e)$ result applies only to residual
completion inside a fixed expansion pool. The model assumes deterministic
processing and transfer times; equipment failures, online arrivals,
rescheduling, and fab-level interactions remain outside its present scope.
Recent module-failure scheduling work further indicates that equipment-state
changes warrant a dedicated extension rather than an untested robustness
claim \citep{zhu2026modulefailure}.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper addresses finite-horizon scheduling of a dual-source mixed-flow
rotating-chamber cluster tool through an operationally explicit two-stage MILP. The
formulation connects product and dummy-wafer routes with mixed
$4\times1$/$2\times2$ recipes, rotary chamber events, robotic transfers,
load-lock states, cleaning, residency, and reusable vacuum-zone dummy wafers. Makespan
is optimized first, after which schedule timing is refined conditional on the
retained discrete structure and primary objective.

The computational contribution is a structure-aware enhancement of HEM. Its
central idea is to exploit information induced by the scheduling model at two
points in the exact solve: SPBS supplies a physically structured incumbent,
and variable-role features distinguish how candidate cuts act on discrete
choices and continuous timing. A3C-inspired parallel policy-gradient
training, bounded beam decoding, and role-submodular completion then allocate
the cut budget, while SCIP retains feasibility and proof responsibilities.

Four verification instances reach zero gap in both stages and satisfy the
independently checked physical constraints. Across the complete 2700-run
nominal matrix, RDMCT-HBS records the lowest observed mean PDI and solve
time, with PDI reductions of 1.41\% relative to adapted HEM and 1.97\%
relative to SCIP, thereby establishing the strongest mean anytime performance
under the evaluated nominal protocol. The instance-level intervals show that
the magnitude of this gain is heterogeneous. Feature reconstruction has the
largest observed mean component reduction, while role completion and
structure-conditioned beam decoding provide additional observed mean
reductions. The independent 960-run SPBS study further shows a 26.19\% PDI
reduction and a rise in incumbent availability from 51.67\% to 100\%, with
both effects significant after Holm adjustment.

The 1500-run stability study further confirms the modeling contribution.
Relative to disabling the second stage, the full formulation reduces
event-reconstructed route-total and route-maximum wait by 51.91\% and 68.89\%,
and cadence CV and cadence-deviation sum by 39.70\% and 94.05\%, with all four
comparisons remaining significant after Holm adjustment. The factorial study
explains where difficulty originates: wafer
count and mixed-recipe composition interact strongly, whereas all prespecified
processing-scale main and interaction confidence intervals
include zero. The 80-run parameter study additionally returns a validated
incumbent in every prescribed one-factor perturbation. Together, these results
establish the operational advantage of the two-stage formulation and a clear
empirical advantage of the structure-aware solver enhancement under the
evaluated protocol.

From an operational perspective, the framework separates throughput,
schedule refinement, and solver guidance without relaxing the rotating-chamber
equipment constraints. Its present scope is deterministic finite-batch
operation, and the wall-clock evidence is tied to the evaluated hardware,
instances, and time limits. Extending the model to stochastic processing,
online arrivals, module failures, and fab-level rescheduling, and evaluating
transfer under larger distribution shifts, are the principal directions for
future work.
