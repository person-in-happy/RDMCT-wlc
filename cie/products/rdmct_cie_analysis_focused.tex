\title{Two-stage MILP scheduling and structure-aware learning-enhanced
branch-and-cut for dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber cluster tools}
\author{Anonymous author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber cluster tools coordinate product and
reusable vacuum-zone dummy wafers across shared chambers, load locks, and
robots. Scheduling couples rotation, cleaning, material availability, and
residence limits; a throughput-optimal sequence can still exhibit poor waits
and cadence. We formulate a finite-horizon two-stage MILP for mixed
$4\times1$/$2\times2$ operation. Same-mode wafer pairs and canonical recipe
slots are fixed before optimization as an instance convention; the MILP
optimizes activation, cleaning, dummy allocation, resource order, and event
timing. Stage one minimizes end-to-end makespan, and stage two retains its
discrete schedule and makespan within numerical tolerance while refining route
waits and chamber cadence. We develop RDMCT-HBS, combining a precomputed
structure-preserving binary-skeleton (SPBS) incumbent, algebraic variable-role
features, A3C-inspired parallel policy-gradient learning, bounded beam
decoding, and structural cut completion inside SCIP. Four exactly solved cases
check internal formulation consistency. Under a common precomputed SPBS,
RDMCT-HBS records the lowest nominal mean primal--dual integral and solution
time among seven methods in 2700 runs; the pairwise differences are not
significant after Holm adjustment. A separate 960-run downstream-solve comparison
associates SPBS with 26.19\% lower mean PDI and an increase in incumbent
availability from 51.67\% to 100\%. Under equal 600-s workflow budgets, 1500
runs show large reductions in route waits and cadence metrics for workflows
with refinement. All 729 larger shifted-instance runs return a solver-checked
incumbent. The evidence supports the formulation's operational value and
documents, without establishing statistical superiority, the lowest observed
nominal means for the proposed search method.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item A two-stage MILP models dual-source mixed rotating-chamber scheduling.
\item Under 600 s, the two-stage workflow yields lower waits and cadence.
\item Precomputed SPBS supplied to SCIP lowers mean PDI by 26.19\%.
\item Role features plus beam decoding attain the lowest nominal mean PDI.
\item All 729 larger shifted-instance runs return solver-checked incumbents.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro-new}

Semiconductor equipment is capital intensive, and its effective capacity is
often determined by scheduling rather than nominal processing speed. Cluster
tools illustrate this dependence particularly clearly: chambers, load locks,
and robotic handlers form a tightly coupled system in which a locally sensible
decision can delay several downstream operations. Better scheduling therefore
offers a direct route to higher asset utilization without additional hardware
\citep{chien2021drlhga,hong2026ddpgct}.

This paper studies a rotating-chamber cluster tool used in a plasma-enhanced
chemical vapor deposition setting. The equipment receives material from two
sources. Product wafers enter from atmospheric load ports, while reusable
vacuum-zone dummy wafers enter from a dedicated storage area. The flows merge
inside shared rotary chambers and compete for vacuum transport. Two recipe
families coexist: $4\times1$ operation processes two chamber sides as a rotary
batch, whereas $2\times2$ operation links consecutive product pairs through
overlapping exposures. Cleaning creates additional dummy-only operations and
splits processing into epochs. A schedule must consequently decide not only
when to process, but also which candidate cycles are active, where to place
cleaning boundaries, which physical dummy wafer to allocate, and how to order
every conflicting robot and module action. In the evaluated formulation,
same-mode wafers are paired by identifier and mapped to fixed canonical
chamber--side slots before optimization. These are fixed instance conventions;
pairing, chamber, and side are therefore not optimized decisions in the
reported instances.

Existing cluster-tool research provides important models for cyclic operation,
multiple wafer types, residency, cleaning, and multi-space modules
\citep{qiao2022binary,li2022conditioncleaning,wang2023optimalcyclic,
li2025cyclicmultitype,yang2025miptwowafers,gu2024multifinger}. Those models do
not jointly represent the finite-horizon dual-source flow, mixed rotary
recipes, reusable dummy inventory, and shared transport considered here.
The difference is structural. A prescribed robot sequence can be evaluated by
an LP, but the present equipment still requires activation, reusable-dummy
assignment, cleaning, resource ordering, and event times to be determined
together for the fixed canonical recipe layout.

Learning-based scheduling offers powerful search guidance, but most studies
act directly on dispatching or process control
\citep{liu2022cnna3c,zhang2024qeda,kim2025adaptivecluster,
kim2025noncyclic}. The closely related CIE study of \citet{hong2026ddpgct}
uses DDPG and discrete-event simulation to adjust continuous processing times
in a PVD cluster tool. Our problem is different: physical processing times are
given, while the method must construct a feasible discrete schedule and retain
valid lower bounds and optimality proofs. This motivates learning inside an
exact solver rather than replacing the solver.

The paper resolves two linked gaps. The modeling gap is addressed by a
two-stage MILP that separates throughput from schedule quality without
separating the physical system. The computational gap is addressed by
embedding equipment structure in both primal initialization and cut selection.
The resulting research logic is direct: the dual-source rotary physics defines
the MILP; the MILP exposes its search bottlenecks; and those bottlenecks define
the information used by the learning-enhanced branch-and-cut method.

The contributions are threefold.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item We formulate a finite-horizon MILP for dual-source mixed-flow
rotating-chamber scheduling under canonical same-mode pairing and slot mapping.
It jointly decides cycle and operation activation, rotary events, robot
transfers, load-lock states, cleaning epochs, residency, reusable vacuum-zone
dummy-wafer allocation, resource order, and event timing.
\item We introduce a makespan-preserving second stage. Unlike makespan-only
scheduling, it explicitly reshapes physical product waits and chamber cadence
within the discrete schedule retained from stage one.
\item We develop \method{}, a structure-aware enhancement of HEM that couples
a feasible binary-skeleton warm start, variable-role cut features,
A3C-inspired parallel learning, bounded beam decoding, and structural set
completion while leaving feasibility and proof to SCIP.
\end{enumerate}

The evidence design links the two contributions to complementary tests. Exact
solves and post-solve checks assess internal formulation consistency; the
nominal comparison measures algorithmic performance; matched contrasts compare
warm-start and refinement workflows; and factorial and shifted-instance
studies characterize computational difficulty and feasible-schedule delivery.

\section{Research background and positioning}
\label{sec:background-new}

\subsection{Cluster-tool scheduling models}

Cluster-tool models have progressed from steady-state robot cycles to richer
representations of product heterogeneity and auxiliary constraints.
Integrated patterns and workload balance have been investigated for dual-arm
tools and multiple wafer types \citep{kim2021integrated,ko2021wafer,
zhu2022twotypes}. Recent MIP and analytical studies coordinate release order,
robot tasks, chamber assignment, and residency for concurrent or multi-type
processing \citep{li2025cyclicmultitype,yang2025miptwowafers}. Cleaning,
purge, and post-processing constraints further demonstrate that temporal
feasibility cannot be inferred from chamber capacity alone
\citep{qiao2022binary,lee2023cleaningplan,yuan2023purge,
zhu2023postprocess,pan2024cycletime}.

Rotating and multi-space modules introduce side-dependent loading and shared
chamber motion. \citet{gu2024multifinger} analyse multi-finger robotic tools
with multi-space process modules, providing the closest physical foundation
for the chamber mechanism studied here. Their predetermined cyclic sequences
do not cover finite mixed batches or a second reusable material stream. The
present problem extends the decision boundary beyond timing a prescribed robot
sequence to jointly selecting cycle activation, dummy identities, cleaning
epochs, resource orders, and event times. Same-mode product pairs and
chamber--side slots remain fixed by the canonical instance construction.

\subsection{Learning-enhanced scheduling and exact optimization}

Deep reinforcement learning has been combined with genetic, adaptive, and
problem-specific search in semiconductor scheduling
\citep{chien2021drlhga,liu2022cnna3c,zhang2024qeda,
li2022hybridfeedback}. These methods show that learned policies are most useful
when coupled with domain structure. Exact optimization adds a complementary
advantage: explicit constraints, primal and dual bounds, and verifiable
feasibility. Recent learning-enhanced MIP research guides branching, primal
search, and cut selection without surrendering those properties
\citep{bengio2021machine,khalil2022mipgnn,scavuzzo2024mlbb}.

Cut selection is especially relevant because valid inequalities differ in
both immediate efficacy and the decisions they constrain. Reinforcement
learning, supervised ranking, look-ahead imitation, and adaptive scoring have
all improved cut management \citep{tang2020cut,huang2022cutranking,
paulus2022looking,turner2023adaptive}. HEM represents selection as a
hierarchical count-and-sequence decision \citep{wang2023hem,wang2024hemtpami}.
However, generic cut features cannot distinguish an inequality acting mainly
on discrete activation from one acting on continuous event timing; greedy
decoding also discards alternative partial sequences. These limitations are
particularly consequential in the proposed MILP because discrete equipment
choices and continuous timing are strongly coupled.

Table~\ref{tab:position-new} summarizes the research position. The novelty is
not the isolated use of MILP, reinforcement learning, or beam search. It is the
integration of a new dual-source rotary scheduling model with a solver method
whose primal and dual guidance are both derived from that model's structure.

\section{Industrial problem and two-stage MILP}

The recipe determines how the flows interact. In $4\times1$ mode, the chamber
loads a front side, rotates, loads a back side, processes both sides, and
unloads them in reverse order. A product-deficient final side is completed by
dummy wafers. In $2\times2$ mode, product pairs form a chain: adjacent
exposures share an intermediate pair, while leading and trailing positions
require dummy pairs. Cleaning interrupts the chain only at a legal exposure
boundary and uses a dummy-only chamber cycle. A physical dummy wafer cannot be
assigned to overlapping chamber occupancies.

This interaction creates three decision layers. The \emph{configuration layer}
chooses active cycles, cleaning epochs, and physical dummy wafers within the
fixed canonical pairing and slot map. The \emph{resource layer} orders all
operations competing for robots, load locks, and chambers. The \emph{timing
layer} assigns event times and enforces residence limits. These layers must be
optimized jointly because activation and dummy reuse change both resource
conflicts and the feasible timing network.

\subsection{Decision structure and principal constraints}

Let $\mathcal W$ denote product wafers, $\mathcal P$ the same-mode product
pairs formed by the canonical preprocessing rule, $\mathcal C$ the rotary
chambers, $\mathcal K$ the ordered candidate recipe slots, $\mathcal O$ the
candidate operations, and $\mathcal R$ the unit-capacity resources.
$\mathcal K(p)$ is the set of slots compatible with pair $p$;
$\mathcal K^-\subseteq\mathcal K$ contains the nonterminal slots, and
$\operatorname{succ}(k)$ is the next slot in the same ordered slot family;
$\mathcal A$ is the technological-arc set, and $\mathcal O_r$ contains the
operations that may use resource $r$. For chamber $c$, $\mathcal J_c^{\rm DW}$
is the set of dummy-wafer demands, $\mathcal T_c$ its physical dummy inventory,
$\mathcal V_c$ its candidate process cycles, and $\mathcal E_c$ its ordered
cleaning epochs. The fixed incidence $\bar x_{pk}\in\{0,1\}$ encodes the
canonical pair-to-slot map. The binary solver variable $x_{pk}$ is fixed to
this incidence and retained for compatibility with downstream activation links.

Binary variables $u_k$, $b_o$, and $b_v$ activate slots, operations, and
candidate process cycles, respectively;
$a_{vce}$ assigns active cycle $v$ to epoch $e$; $z_{ce}$ activates an epoch;
$g_{j\tau}$ assigns dummy demand $j$ to physical dummy $\tau$; and
$y^r_{oo'}$ orders two operations sharing resource $r$. The activation $q_j$
of dummy demand $j$ is linked to its generating cycle. Each operation $o$ has
start $s_o$, completion $e_o$, active duration $d_ob_o$, and fixed duration
$d_o>0$. Constants $\delta_{oo'}$ and $\rho^r_{oo'}$ are technological and
endpoint-dependent setup separations, and $M$ is a scheduling-horizon bound.
For readability, the following equations state the principal families rather
than every event-specific route and rotary equality:
\begin{align}
&\sum_{k\in\mathcal K(p)}\bar x_{pk}=1
&&p\in\mathcal P, \label{eq:new-assign}\\
&x_{pk}=\bar x_{pk}
&&p\in\mathcal P,\ k\in\mathcal K(p), \label{eq:new-fixed-map}\\
&x_{pk}\le u_k
&&p\in\mathcal P,\ k\in\mathcal K(p), \label{eq:new-map-activate}\\
&u_{\operatorname{succ}(k)}\le u_k
&&k\in\mathcal K^-, \label{eq:new-activate}\\
&e_o=s_o+d_ob_o &&o\in\mathcal O, \label{eq:new-duration}\\
&s_{o'}\ge e_o+\delta_{oo'}-M(2-b_o-b_{o'})
&&(o,o')\in\mathcal A, \label{eq:new-prec}\\
&s_{o'}\ge e_o+\rho^r_{oo'}-M(1-y^r_{oo'})-M(2-b_o-b_{o'}),
&&r\in\mathcal R,\ \{o,o'\}\subseteq\mathcal O_r,\label{eq:new-res1}\\
&s_o\ge e_{o'}+\rho^r_{o'o}-My^r_{oo'}-M(2-b_o-b_{o'}),
&&r\in\mathcal R,\ \{o,o'\}\subseteq\mathcal O_r,\label{eq:new-res2}\\
&\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T_c}g_{j\tau}=q_j
&&c\in\mathcal C,\ j\in\mathcal J_c^{\rm DW}.\label{eq:new-dummy}
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:new-assign}--\eqref{eq:new-fixed-map} disclose the fixed
canonical slot map rather than an endogenous grouping decision.
Equations~\eqref{eq:new-map-activate}--\eqref{eq:new-activate} link that map to
a contiguous active structure, while
Eq.~\eqref{eq:new-duration} makes activation consistent with event timing.
Technological arcs in Eq.~\eqref{eq:new-prec} create active product and dummy
routes. The activation-qualified disjunctions
\eqref{eq:new-res1}--\eqref{eq:new-res2} prevent overlap on the ATR, aligner,
load-lock slots, VTR, chambers, and each reusable dummy wafer; $\rho$ includes
endpoint-dependent robot repositioning.

mathematical role. This mapping is important because feasibility is a property
of the complete equipment trajectory, not only of chamber occupancy.

Cleaning epochs satisfy
\begin{align}
&\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_c}a_{vce}=b_v
&&c\in\mathcal C,\ v\in\mathcal V_c, \label{eq:new-epoch-assign}\\
&a_{vce}\le z_{ce},\qquad
\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_c}a_{vce}\le H z_{ce}
&&c\in\mathcal C,\ e\in\mathcal E_c, \label{eq:new-epoch-cap}\\
&z_{c,e+1}\le z_{ce}
&&c\in\mathcal C,\ e,e+1\in\mathcal E_c, \label{eq:new-epoch-order}
\end{align}
where $H$ is the maximum number of active process cycles between cleans.
Linking constraints insert a dummy-only clean between consecutive active
epochs and allow a $2\times2$ chain to split only at an exposure boundary.
Residence limits take the form
\begin{equation}
0\le s_{\mathrm{out}(w,r)}-e_{\mathrm{in}(w,r)}\le \overline R_{wr}.
\end{equation}

In cluster-tool control, deadlock is a reachable equipment state in which
wafer--resource dependencies form a circular wait; Petri-net formulations
address this controller-level reachability property explicitly
\citep{nishi2015deadlock}. The present
finite-horizon MILP makes a narrower, offline-schedule claim. Every active
route has a finite source-to-return precedence chain, and every active pair
sharing a unit-capacity resource receives one temporal order. Provided all physical hold
intervals and transfers are represented and active separations are positive,
a directed cycle in the combined route--resource graph would make the summed
timing inequalities imply $0>0$ and would therefore be infeasible. Terminal constraints require
every product and assigned dummy wafer to return. Thus a feasible solution is a
complete scheduled trajectory without circular wait under the modeled
conditions; this is not a controller-level deadlock-freeness guarantee under
dispatch deviations, unmodeled buffers, or equipment failures.

\subsection{Throughput first, schedule quality second}

Stage one minimizes the return time of the last product wafer:
\begin{equation}
\cmax\ge e_w^{\rm return}\quad(w\in\mathcal W),\qquad
\min_{\xi\in\mathcal F}\cmax(\xi),
\label{eq:new-stage1}
\end{equation}
where $\xi$ collects the model variables and $\mathcal F$ contains all
activation, canonical-map, rotary, cleaning, dummy-allocation, resource, and
timing constraints. A makespan objective alone is insufficient operationally.
Once the last return time is fixed, many schedules may be equivalent in
$\cmax$ but differ substantially in intermediate product waiting, extreme
holds, and chamber cadence. Those differences affect exposure consistency,
work-in-process residence, and recovery from small disturbances.

Let $\widehat\xi$ be the best stage-one schedule and $\widehat C$ its makespan,
and let $\mathcal D_{\rm fix}$ index the discrete variables retained from that
incumbent. Stage two imposes
\begin{equation}
\xi_d=\widehat\xi_d\ (d\in\mathcal D_{\rm fix}),\qquad
\cmax(\xi)\le \widehat C+\varepsilon_C.
\label{eq:new-fix}
\end{equation}
It then minimizes
\begin{equation}
\Phi(\xi)=\lambda_1 L_{\rm sum}+\lambda_2 L_{\max}
+\lambda_3 D_{\rm cadence}+\lambda_4 E_{\rm cap},
\label{eq:new-stage2}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_\alpha\ge0$ ($\alpha\in\mathcal Q$) are the model-native wait
and idle variables, and $h_{mce,r}$ is the $r$th active processing start in the
retained order for recipe mode $m$, chamber $c$, and cleaning epoch $e$. The
absolute values, maximum, and positive parts in the operational description
are implemented with the following linear epigraph relations, which are tight
for the positively weighted terms at an optimum:
\begin{align}
&L_{\rm sum}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal Q}\omega_\alpha,
\qquad L_{\max}\ge\omega_\alpha &&\alpha\in\mathcal Q,
\label{eq:new-wait-epi}\\
&v_{mce,r}\ge (h_{mce,r+1}-h_{mce,r})
 -(h_{mce,r}-h_{mce,r-1}), &&(m,c,e,r)\in\mathcal I_h,
\label{eq:new-cad-pos}\\
&v_{mce,r}\ge -(h_{mce,r+1}-h_{mce,r})
 +(h_{mce,r}-h_{mce,r-1}), &&(m,c,e,r)\in\mathcal I_h,
\label{eq:new-cad-neg}\\
&D_{\rm cadence}=\sum_{(m,c,e,r)\in\mathcal I_h}v_{mce,r},
\qquad v_{mce,r}\ge0,\label{eq:new-cad-sum}\\
&\eta_\alpha\ge\omega_\alpha-\bar\omega_\alpha,
\quad\eta_\alpha\ge0,\qquad
E_{\rm cap}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal Q}\eta_\alpha.
\label{eq:new-cap-epi}
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal I_h$ contains the interior indices $r=2,\ldots,n_{mce}-1$
of retained chamber-start sequences with $n_{mce}\ge3$, and
$\bar\omega_\alpha$ is the configured soft wait cap.
The nonnegative weights $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_4$ set the trade-off among
aggregate waiting, extreme waiting, cadence irregularity, and soft wait-cap
excess. The formal linear profile uses weights $(1,10,25,0)$; the last term is
disabled and not interpreted when its weight is zero; it is tight when a
positive soft-cap weight is configured. Physical product-route
waits reported as operational outcomes are reconstructed uniformly from event
times, rather than read from these objective auxiliaries; their six-segment
definition is given in the supplement.

Here $\varepsilon_C$ is only the numerical equality tolerance.
Equation~\eqref{eq:new-fix} gives the two-stage model a clear guarantee. The
second stage cannot worsen the retained makespan beyond this tolerance and cannot change the chosen
activation, cleaning, dummy-allocation, or resource-order structure. If stage
one proves optimality, the refined schedule remains globally makespan-optimal
within the same tolerance. If stage two also proves optimality, its timing is
optimal for Eq.~\eqref{eq:new-stage2} within the retained discrete structure;
otherwise the returned refinement is feasible under the makespan cap but is not
claimed to be stage-two optimal. If stage one ends with an incumbent, makespan
retention is relative to that incumbent. The model therefore separates
objectives without disconnecting them from the same physical schedule.

\subsection{Modeling advance}

The formulation extends existing rotating or multi-space models in four
directions simultaneously: finite rather than steady-state scheduling; two
material sources rather than one; joint activation, cleaning, dummy-allocation,
resource-order, and timing decisions on a canonical mixed-recipe layout; and
conditional schedule-quality refinement rather than makespan alone. This
combination matters because dummy availability, cleaning, and active rotary
cycles alter the robot conflict graph itself. They cannot be appended as
independent capacity constraints after a complete product trajectory has been
timed.

The same structure explains computational difficulty. Activation, epoch,
dummy identity, and pairwise order binaries interact with a dense event
time network. The resulting cuts may affect different algebraic roles even
when their generic efficacy scores are similar. This observation motivates
the solver design in the next section.

\section{Structure-aware learning-enhanced exact solution}
\label{sec:method-new}

\subsection{From model structure to solver guidance}

The MILP exhibits two practical symptoms: finding the first competitive
incumbent can be slow, and generic cut scores do not reveal whether a cut
tightens discrete equipment choices, continuous timing, or their coupling.
\method{} addresses the symptoms at complementary points. Structure-preserving
binary-skeleton (SPBS) initialization supplies
primal guidance before branch-and-cut, while the learned selector supplies
dual guidance during separation. Both components remain advisory: SCIP keeps
the original feasible region, validates incumbents, generates valid cuts, and
retains proof responsibility.

\subsection{Structure-preserving binary-skeleton warm start}

SPBS retains the canonical pair-to-slot equalities and fixes candidate
activation, cleaning-epoch, load-lock, and selected resource-order roles in a
temporary feasibility MIP. If a profile finds no incumbent, VTR, load-lock,
and other order fixings are progressively released; continuous event times
always remain free. A start is accepted only after reconstruction and complete
feasibility checking in the full model. Only that feasible schedule---not the
temporary fixing constraints---is injected into the unrestricted production
model. Formal evaluations use this incumbent after it has been precomputed.

This design is stronger than an arbitrary primal hint. The retained decisions
encode the combinatorial architecture that the detailed timing model must
otherwise discover from scratch, while the completion solve repairs local
timing interactions. Because the original model receives only a feasible
incumbent, SPBS cannot remove a better solution or weaken an optimality proof.

\subsection{Variable-role reconstruction and hierarchical decoding}

HEM describes candidate cuts with 13 generic statistics. We add ten structural
features derived from solver metadata. Variables are partitioned into binary,
general integer, objective-active continuous, auxiliary continuous, and other
roles. For cut $i$, the coefficient mass on role $g$ is
\begin{equation}
p_{ig}=\frac{\sum_{j:x_j\in g}|a_{ij}|}
{\sum_j|a_{ij}|+\varepsilon}.
\label{eq:new-role}
\end{equation}
Role masses, bounded-variable mass, coefficient sign balance,
discrete--continuous coupling, dominant-role mass, and role entropy extend the
representation to 23 dimensions. The features are invariant to variable names
and distinguish broad algebraic variable domains. They do not, by themselves,
identify equipment semantics among variables in the same domain. Candidates
are screened deterministically by efficacy to at most 128 rows, and the
selector returns at most 16 cuts.

The 13 generic and 10 role features are standardized with the training
statistics stored with each trained parameter set. Layer normalization,
separate nonlinear
projections of the two blocks, sigmoid-gated fusion, a one-layer LSTM, and one
pointer-attention glimpse produce the policy state. The LSTM hidden size is
128 and the tanh exploration constant is 5. The high-level output is a
continuous selection fraction capped at 0.2; its discrete cardinality is
obtained by flooring the candidate count times that fraction, applying a
minimum-two safeguard when feasible, and enforcing the 16-cut cap.

A high-level policy selects the cut budget and a low-level pointer policy
orders candidates \citep{vinyals2015pointer}. If $k$ cuts are selected from
state $S$, their joint probability is
\begin{equation}
\pi_\theta(k,i_{1:k}\mid S)=\pi_\theta^{H}(k\mid S)
\prod_{t=1}^{k}\pi_\theta^{L}(i_t\mid S,i_{1:t-1}).
\end{equation}
Two workers collect independent SCIP trajectories in parallel; their batches
then drive a synchronous central update of the shared policy with return
$R=-\pdi$. The
estimator
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\nabla_\theta J}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}
(R_b-\bar R_{\rm EMA})\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(\tau_b),\qquad
\bar R_{\rm EMA}\leftarrow0.9\bar R_{\rm EMA}+0.1\bar R
\end{equation}
uses an exponential-moving-average return baseline, not an independently
learned critic. It is therefore hierarchical REINFORCE
\citep{williams1992reinforce} with A3C-inspired parallel trajectory collection,
not the asynchronous actor--critic algorithm of \citet{mnih2016a3c}. Training
uses five training instances and five seeds. For each seed, two workers collect
eight 30-s/1024-MB trajectories per epoch for 60 epochs; Adam learning rates
are $10^{-4}$ and $5\times10^{-4}$ for the low- and high-level policies.
For each family and seed, evaluation uses the predetermined terminal-epoch
(epoch 60) parameter set; the candidate count is one. Eligibility is checked
only from the training path, seed, PDI reward, feature dimension, and epoch; no
benchmark- or test-based parameter selection is performed.

At inference, width-three beam search ranks partial sequences by cumulative
joint log probability without length normalization, rather than committing to
the first locally best cut \citep{choo2022beam}. Role-submodular completion
then adds candidates that balance policy score, immediate cut quality, role
coverage, and representation of the remaining pool
\citep{nemhauser1978analysis}. The
mechanisms are complementary: reconstruction supplies meaningful diversity;
beam decoding explores alternative orders; completion prevents the final set
from collapsing onto one variable role.

\subsection{Exactness and computational scope}

\method{} ranks only cuts already generated and certified as valid by SCIP
\citep{achterberg2009scip,bestuzheva2023scip}.
SPBS adds only a solver-checked incumbent. Consequently, neither component
changes the objective, constraints, integrality requirements, or lower-bound
logic. The method can improve anytime behavior while preserving the same
mathematical meaning of feasibility and optimality as default SCIP. The
supplementary material documents the reproducibility protocol.

\section{Computational results and analysis}
\label{sec:results-new}

\subsection{Evidence structure}

The study uses seven complementary evidence sets. Four exactly solved cases
check internal model consistency. A 2700-run nominal matrix compares seven
methods over 20 manufacturing instances and independent solver and training
realizations. A 960-run matched experiment compares downstream SCIP solves
with a precomputed SPBS incumbent against solves without that incumbent. A
1500-run study compares three equal-total-budget workflows: the two refinement
profiles use 540 s for the primary solve and up to 60 s for refinement, whereas
the no-refinement profile uses all 600 s for the primary solve. A 300-run
factorial design estimates associations of wafer count, recipe composition,
and process scale with difficulty. An 80-run one-factor study assesses
solvability under parameter perturbations. Finally, 729 runs on nine shifted
40--64-wafer configurations assess larger-instance feasible-schedule delivery.
All saved incumbents supporting reported conclusions pass SCIP's check and a
post-solve feasibility check against the same serialized MILP.

PDI is the primary anytime metric because it integrates SCIP's normalized
primal--dual gap over the common horizon; lower is better. Terminal gap is the
normalized bound gap at termination. Reported solution time is the sum of
SCIP-reported primary and conditional-refinement solve times; it excludes model
loading, learned-parameter loading, solution serialization, instance
construction, and offline SPBS generation. Repetitions are aggregated
within manufacturing instances before paired bootstrap intervals, Wilcoxon
tests, and Holm adjustment are calculated. This prevents repeated random seeds
from being treated as independent manufacturing evidence
\citep{gleixner2021miplib}. Hardware, budgets, hyperparameters, complete
matrix definitions, and validation tolerances are reported in the supplement.

\subsection{Industrial implications}

Three implications follow from the evidence. First, the prespecified two-stage
workflows have substantially better wait and cadence metrics than the
equal-total-budget no-refinement workflow, subject to the 540+60 versus 600-s
primary-search confound. Second, supplying a precomputed application-feasible
incumbent gives the largest statistically supported computational contrast
measured here, although its offline construction cost is outside that
comparison. Third, the numerical component pattern motivates representing a
cut's algebraic variable domains. The observed sign reversal of the beam
contrast is consistent with coupling between beam search and role information,
but neither the individual module contrasts nor their interaction are
statistically established.

The explicit state, resource, and timing layers also provide a possible foundation
for online arrivals, stochastic processing, and module-failure recourse. The
present finite-batch evidence shows that, on the tested canonical-slot
instances, equal-budget two-stage workflows improve the reported operational
metrics and \method{} attains the lowest observed nominal mean PDI and time
among the compared methods without a significant pairwise advantage.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion-new}

This paper develops an integrated model-and-algorithm framework for
dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber cluster tools. The two-stage MILP
represents product wafers, reusable vacuum-zone dummy wafers, mixed rotary
recipes, cleaning, shared robots, load locks, and residence restrictions under
fixed canonical same-mode pairing and chamber--side slots. Its first stage
optimizes end-to-end makespan; its second stage retains the discrete production
plan and refines its timing quality.

Four exactly solved cases and post-solve checks establish internal consistency
for the tested instances. Relative to the 600-s no-refinement workflow, the
540+60-s two-stage workflow has 51.91\%, 68.89\%, 39.70\%, and 94.05\% lower
route-total wait, maximum route wait, cadence variation, and cadence deviation;
this equal-budget comparison does not isolate refinement conditional on an
identical primary incumbent. \method{} records the lowest observed mean PDI and
solution time in the 2700-run seven-method comparison, but its pairwise
advantages are not significant after adjustment. Supplying precomputed SPBS is
associated with 26.19\% lower downstream-solve PDI and 100\% incumbent
availability; construction time is excluded. The factorial study identifies
the largest estimated PDI terms as scale--recipe-mixture interactions within
the tested design. All 729 OOD runs produce solver-checked incumbents on nine
larger shifted configurations, although none proves optimality.

The results support the operational usefulness of the prespecified two-stage
workflow and document the best observed nominal mean anytime performance for
the structure-aware learning-enhanced branch-and-cut method in this comparison.
They do not establish universal superiority beyond the tested canonical-slot
instances, budgets, and distributions.
