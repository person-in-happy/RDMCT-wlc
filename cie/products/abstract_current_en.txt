PW-pair-based two-stage MILP scheduling and structured solution methods for dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber cluster tools

Dual-source mixed-flow rotating-chamber cluster tools require full occupancy during processing and cleaning, with reusable auxiliary wafers filling otherwise vacant positions. We formulate a two-stage mixed-integer linear program using two-wafer pairs to connect handling operations and chamber cycles, and batch inventory variables to recover individual wafer identities. The formulation jointly determines pairing, chamber assignment, cleaning, auxiliary-wafer reuse, and resource sequences. The first stage minimizes product-return makespan; the second retains its upper bound and principal discrete structure while refining waiting and cadence. A structured initialization procedure is combined with five-level binary-skeleton improvement and feature and inference extensions to hierarchical learning-based cut selection. Under the default 100-s nonprocessing residence and robot holding limits, four of eight small instances have certified primary optima and one yields a feasible time-limited schedule. In 180 conditional algorithm runs, initialization reduces the two-wafer mixed-instance makespan from 518 to 505 s and the four-wafer mixed-instance makespan from 542 to 541 s under root separation. Learned-cut rankings change with the instance and separation protocol. Another 36 structural comparisons support exploiting existing schedules before joint search, whereas 12 completion-tail comparisons yield no improvement. Historical extended-window experiments support conditional timing refinement but are reported separately. The evidence supports verifiable wafer decoding and conditional initialization benefits; it does not establish consistent cut-selection superiority, large-scale scalability, or end-to-end acceleration.
