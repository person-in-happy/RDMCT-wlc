Abstract

Rotating-chamber cluster tools are equipment-level manufacturing systems
in which wafer ow, chamber rotation, cleaning, reusable dummy wafers,
and shared robots must be coordinated without intermediate buering. This
study develops a source-congurable scheduling framework that separates
external material interfaces from a common internal congurationresource
timing model. A single-source tool is the base specialization; a dual-source
mixed-ow 4×1/2×2 conguration is used as the strongly coupled benchmark.
The nite-horizon two-stage mixed-integer linear program rst minimizes
end-to-end makespan and then preserves the selected discrete schedule and
makespan within numerical tolerance while rening physical route waits and
chamber cadence. Exact solution is enhanced by a solver-checked structure-
preserving binary-skeleton (SPBS) warm start and RDMCT-HBS, which
combines variable-role cut features, hierarchical learning, bounded beam
decoding, and structural cut completion inside SCIP. Four exactly solved
cases check internal model consistency. Across 1500 equal-600-s workow
runs, the two-stage workow reduces total route wait, maximum route wait,
cadence variation, and cadence deviation by 51.91%, 68.89%, 39.70%, and
94.05%, respectively. In 2700 nominal runs sharing the same SPBS incum-
bent, RDMCT-HBS records the lowest mean primaldual integral and solu-
tion time among seven solver congurations. A separate 960-run paired com-

     1. Introduction

         Semiconductor manufacturing depends on capital-intensive, highly auto-
     mated process equipment. Equipment utilization and production cadence de-
     pend not only on nominal processing times, but also on coordination among
 5   wafer transport, load-lock switching, process-chamber occupancy, and control
     logic. A cluster tool (CT) integrates multiple process modules, load locks,
     and wafer handlers in a single controlled environment. Because intermedi-
     ate buers are limited or absent, robot tasks and wafer processing must be
     scheduled jointly; residency-time limits, chamber cleaning, and activity-time
10   variation further shrink the feasible scheduling space (Pan et al., 2018). CT
     scheduling is therefore not a generic parallel-machine sequencing problem,
     but an equipment-level manufacturing-system coordination problem.
         Photolithography, etching, and deposition are three central stages of semi-
     conductor manufacturing. Within thin-lm deposition, plasma-enhanced
15   chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) that supports advanced processes is
     a major focus of semiconductor equipment development. As throughput and
     oor-space utilization requirements rise, conventional CTs no longer meet
     the full complexity of PECVD operation. Equipment manufacturers there-
     fore place multiple processing spaces inside a module and introduce chamber
20   rotation, allowing several wafers to be processed while preserving robotic
     access to every station. For a four-space rotary chamber, Gu et al. (2024)
     showed that rotation changes which spaces are accessible, so loading order,
     rotation times, and wafer residency must be coordinated.
         Carbon-containing lms are also dicult to clean, and the multi-space
25   rotary chamber considered here must avoid empty stations and operate with
     all positions occupied. Manufacturers address this requirement by protecting
     otherwise vacant stations with reusable dummy wafers. The dummy-wafer
     storage module is mounted directly in the vacuum domain, where dummy

     of which 4 × 1 parallel processing and 2 × 2 chained processing are the two

     activation, physical dummy identities, permitted robot and load-lock choices,

50       Existing cluster-tool research covers cyclic schedules, multiple wafer types,
     residency limits, cleaning, and multi-space modules (Qiao et al., 2022; Li
     et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023a; Li and Yang, 2025; Yang et al., 2025; Gu
     et al., 2024). Yet rotary nite-batch operation still lacks a unied exact rep-
     resentation of dual-source ow, mixed recipes, reusable dummy wafers, and
55   shared transport. This gap has direct operational consequences: changing
     one dummy assignment or cleaning boundary simultaneously rewrites the
     robot-conict graph and the continuous timing network.
         Learning-based scheduling usually controls dispatching or process param-
     eters directly (Liu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2024; Kim and Lee, 2025; Kim
60   and Kim, 2025). In a related equipment setting, Hong et al. (2026) combine
     DDPG and discrete-event simulation to adjust continuous processing times
     in a PVD cluster tool. Our decision layer is dierent. Processing times are
     given, and the algorithm must deliver a complete discrete rotary schedule
     while retaining MIP feasibility checks, lower bounds, and optimality proofs.
65   Learning is therefore used only to improve search order inside the exact solver,
     not to replace the physical constraint model.
         This design carries equipment structure through three layers: formulation,
     initialization, and cutting planes. The two-stage MILP rst determines end-
     to-end throughput and then improves route waits and chamber cadence while
70   preserving the selected discrete structure and makespan. SPBS constructs
     a binary skeleton that the full MILP can verify. RDMCT-HBS then uses
     variable roles, hierarchical selection, and bounded beam search to improve
     nite-time branch-and-cut. The novelty is not the isolated use of MILP, warm
     starts, or learned cut selection, but their use of the same rotary-chamber
75   physics.
         The contributions are threefold.

     1. We formulate a source-congurable nite-horizon MILP for rotary cluster
        tools. It separates external material topology from internal conguration
        resourcetiming decisions and, under canonical same-mode pairing and
80      xed chamber slots, jointly represents rotation, robots, load locks, cleaning
        epochs, residency, and reusable vacuum-zone dummy wafers. The dual-
        source mixed-ow conguration activates the strongest materialresource
        coupling in the computational study.

                                            4
      2. We introduce a throughput-rst, operational-quality-second mechanism.
 85      Stage two preserves the discrete structure and makespan bound selected
         by stage one, and adjusts only the event times that remain free. It there-
         fore places nishing on time and nishing with less waiting and steadier
         cadence in the same veriable schedule.
      3. We develop complementary structure-aware exact-search mechanisms.
 90      SPBS improves primal-solution acquisition; RDMCT-HBS combines
         variable-role features, A3C-inspired parallel training, bounded beam
         decoding, and structured cut completion to improve dual search. Both
         work through standard SCIP interfaces and leave the MILP feasible
         region unchanged.

 95       Each claim is tied to independent evidence. Four small instances reach
      zero gap and pass post-solve checks. Under the same 600-s total budget, the
      two-stage workow reduces total route wait, maximum route wait, cadence
      variation, and cadence deviation by 51.91%, 68.89%, 39.70%, and 94.05%, re-
      spectively. SPBS raises incumbent availability from 51.67% to 100%. Among
100   seven nominal congurations sharing the same SPBS incumbent, RDMCT-
      HBS records the lowest observed mean PDI and mean solution time. All
      729 shifted runs with 4064 wafers return solver-checked feasible schedules.
      Within the tested instances and budgets, these results support two practi-
      cal advantages: earlier delivery of complete executable schedules and better
105   equipment-level operating quality without throughput degradation.

      2. Research background and positioning

      2.1. Cluster-tool scheduling models
          Cluster-tool models have progressed from steady-state robot cycles to
      richer representations of product heterogeneity and auxiliary constraints. In-
110   tegrated patterns and workload balance have been investigated for dual-arm
      tools and multiple wafer types (Kim et al., 2021; Ko et al., 2021; Zhu et al.,
      2022). Recent MIP and analytical studies coordinate release order, robot
      tasks, chamber assignment, and residency for concurrent or multi-type pro-
      cessing (Li and Yang, 2025; Yang et al., 2025). Cleaning, purge, and post-
115   processing constraints further demonstrate that temporal feasibility cannot
      be inferred from chamber capacity alone (Qiao et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2023;
      Yuan et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023; Pan et al., 2024).


                                            5
          Rotating and multi-space modules introduce side-dependent loading and
      shared chamber motion. Gu et al. (2024) analyse multi-nger robotic tools
120   with multi-space process modules, providing the closest physical foundation
      for the chamber mechanism studied here. Their predetermined cyclic se-
      quences do not cover nite mixed batches or a second reusable material
      stream. The present problem extends the decision boundary beyond tim-
      ing a prescribed robot sequence to jointly selecting cycle activation, dummy
125   identities, cleaning epochs, resource orders, and event times. Same-mode
      product pairs and chamberside slots remain xed by the canonical instance
      construction.

      2.2. Learning-enhanced scheduling and exact optimization
          Deep reinforcement learning has been combined with genetic, adaptive,
130   and problem-specic search in semiconductor scheduling (Chien and Lan,
      2021; Liu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2024; Li and Chang, 2022). These
      methods show that learned policies are most useful when coupled with do-
      main structure. Exact optimization adds a complementary advantage: ex-
      plicit constraints, primal and dual bounds, and veriable feasibility. Recent
135   learning-enhanced MIP research guides branching, primal search, and cut
      selection without surrendering those properties (Bengio et al., 2021; Khalil
      et al., 2022; Scavuzzo et al., 2024).
          Cut selection is especially relevant because valid inequalities dier in both
      immediate ecacy and the decisions they constrain. Reinforcement learning,
140   supervised ranking, look-ahead imitation, and adaptive scoring have all im-
      proved cut management (Tang et al., 2020; Huang et al., 2022; Paulus et al.,
      2022; Turner et al., 2023). HEM represents selection as a hierarchical count-
      and-sequence decision (Wang et al., 2023b, 2024). However, generic cut fea-
      tures cannot distinguish an inequality acting mainly on discrete activation
145   from one acting on continuous event timing; greedy decoding also discards al-
      ternative partial sequences. These limitations are particularly consequential
      in the proposed MILP because discrete equipment choices and continuous
      timing are strongly coupled.
          Table 1 summarizes the research position. The novelty is not the isolated
150   use of MILP, reinforcement learning, or beam search. It is the integration of
      a source-congurable rotary scheduling model with a solver method whose
      primal and dual guidance are both derived from that model's structure.

      3.4. Modeling advance
          The formulation extends existing rotating or multi-space models in four
      directions simultaneously: nite rather than steady-state scheduling; a cong-

                                             13
      urable source interface whose single-source base admits a dual-source mixed-
280   ow specialization; joint activation, cleaning, dummy-allocation, resource-
      order, and timing decisions on a canonical mixed-recipe layout; and condi-
      tional schedule-quality renement rather than makespan alone. This combi-
      nation matters because source topology, dummy availability, cleaning, and
      active rotary cycles alter the robot conict graph itself. They cannot be
285   appended as independent capacity constraints after a complete product tra-
      jectory has been timed.
          The same structure explains computational diculty. Activation, epoch,
      dummy identity, and pairwise order binaries interact with a dense event time
      network. The resulting cuts may aect dierent algebraic roles even when
290   their generic ecacy scores are similar. This observation motivates the solver
      design in the next section.

      4. Structure-aware learning-enhanced exact solution

      4.1. From model structure to solver guidance
          The MILP exhibits two practical symptoms: nding the rst competi-
295   tive incumbent can be slow, and generic cut scores do not reveal whether a
      cut tightens discrete equipment choices, continuous timing, or their coupling.
      RDMCT-HBS addresses the symptoms at complementary points. Structure-
      preserving binary-skeleton (SPBS) initialization supplies primal guidance be-
      fore branch-and-cut, while the learned selector supplies dual guidance during
300   separation. Both components remain advisory: SCIP keeps the original fea-
      sible region, validates incumbents, generates valid cuts, and retains proof
      responsibility.

      4.2. Structure-preserving binary-skeleton warm start
          SPBS retains the canonical pair-to-slot equalities and xes candidate acti-
305   vation, cleaning-epoch, load-lock, and selected resource-order roles in a tem-
      porary feasibility MIP. If a prole nds no incumbent, VTR, load-lock, and
      other order xings are progressively released; continuous event times always
      remain free. A start is accepted only after reconstruction and complete fea-
      sibility checking in the full model. Only that feasible schedulenot the tem-
310   porary xing constraintsis injected into the unrestricted production model.
      Formal evaluations use this incumbent after it has been precomputed.
          This design is stronger than an arbitrary primal hint. The retained deci-
      sions encode the combinatorial architecture that the detailed timing model

                                            14
      must otherwise discover from scratch, while the completion solve repairs lo-
315   cal timing interactions. Because the original model receives only a feasible
      incumbent, SPBS cannot remove a better solution or weaken an optimality
      proof.

      4.4. Exactness and computational scope
365       RDMCT-HBS ranks only cuts already generated and certied as valid by
      SCIP (Achterberg, 2009; Bestuzheva et al., 2023). SPBS adds only a solver-
      checked incumbent. Consequently, neither component changes the objective,
      constraints, integrality requirements, or lower-bound logic. The method can
      improve anytime behavior while preserving the same mathematical meaning
370   of feasibility and optimality as default SCIP. The supplementary material
      documents the reproducibility protocol.

      5.9. Industrial implications
525       Three implications follow from the evidence. First, the prespecied two-
      stage workows have substantially better wait and cadence metrics than the
      equal-total-budget no-renement workow, subject to the 540+60 versus 600-

      feasible incumbent gives the largest statistically supported computational
530   contrast measured here, although its oine construction cost is outside that
      comparison. Third, the numerical component pattern motivates representing
      a cut's algebraic variable domains. The observed sign reversal of the beam
      contrast is consistent with coupling between beam search and role informa-
      tion, but neither the individual module contrasts nor their interaction are
535   statistically established.
          The explicit state, resource, and timing layers also provide a possible foun-
      dation for online arrivals, stochastic processing, and module-failure recourse.
      The present nite-batch evidence shows that, on the tested canonical-slot in-
      stances, equal-budget two-stage workows improve the reported operational
540   metrics and RDMCT-HBS attains the lowest observed nominal mean PDI
      and time among the compared methods without a signicant pairwise advan-
      tage.

      6. Conclusions

          This paper develops an integrated model-and-algorithm framework for a
545   class of rotating-chamber semiconductor cluster tools. The source interface
      separates external material topology from a common internal conguration
      resourcetiming model: single-source operation is the base specialization, and
      the dual-source mixed-ow conguration is the evaluated stress case. The
      two-stage MILP represents product wafers, reusable vacuum-zone dummy
550   wafers, mixed rotary recipes, cleaning, shared robots, load locks, and res-
      idence restrictions under xed canonical same-mode pairing and chamber


                                             25
      side slots. Its rst stage optimizes end-to-end makespan; its second stage
      retains the discrete production plan and renes its timing quality.
          Four exactly solved cases and post-solve checks establish internal consis-
555   tency for the tested instances. Relative to the 600-s no-renement workow,
      the 540+60-s two-stage workow has 51.91%, 68.89%, 39.70%, and 94.05%
      lower route-total wait, maximum route wait, cadence variation, and cadence
      deviation; this equal-budget comparison does not isolate renement condi-
      tional on an identical primary incumbent. RDMCT-HBS records the lowest
560   observed mean PDI and solution time in the 2700-run seven-method compar-
      ison, but its pairwise advantages are not signicant after adjustment. Sup-
      plying precomputed SPBS is associated with 26.19% lower downstream-solve
      PDI and 100% incumbent availability; construction time is excluded. The
      factorial study identies the largest estimated PDI terms as scalerecipe-
565   mixture interactions within the tested design. All 729 OOD runs produce
      solver-checked incumbents on nine larger shifted congurations, although
      none proves optimality.
          The results support the operational usefulness of the prespecied two-
      stage workow and document the lowest observed nominal mean anytime per-
570   formance for the structure-aware learning-enhanced branch-and-cut method
      in this comparison. The scope of inference is the tested canonical-slot in-
      stances, budgets, and distributions.
